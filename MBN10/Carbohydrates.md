
Carbs can indirectly affect fat storage but supplying the energy that gets burned instead of the fat that was supposed to be burned for energy. 

*This is called reduction in fat oxidation*

For example if we consume 80g of fat and burn 80g of fat, we'll have 0 fat left over but if we were to add in carbs, we would eat 80g of fat, burn 80g of fat and have 30g leftover

DNL: making more fatty acids (TG) from nonfat precursors like carbs

### Alcohol
Produces acetate that can be used for energy (converted to calories, not necessarily feel more energized).

Fuel priority: 
Acetate > Carbs > Fatty Acids
*Indicates which takes precedence, but doesn't mean it completely burns one then moves to the next*

Overloading on one, displaces anothers oxidation, increasing fat storage.
