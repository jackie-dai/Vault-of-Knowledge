
Macronutrients (big)
- protein
	- consist of amino acids (AA)
	- involved in growth, maintenance, and repair
	- meat, fish, poultry, dairy, legumes, and grains provide high protein
- carbs
	- two types: simple sugars (sucrose, lactose) or complex carbs (grains, beans, peas, potatoes)
	- certain complex carbs promote better blood glucose control than simple sugars
- fat
	- triacglyglycerol (TAG) or triglyceride (TG) consists of fatty acids bound to glcerol
	- dense energy storage (more than protein and carbs) ~9kcal/g
- water 

Micronutrients (small)
- vitamins
	- organic chemicals serving as cofactors for metabolic reactions
	- regulate metabolism of other dietary chemicals
	- classified as either fat soluble (vitamin A, D, E, K) or water soluble (vitamins B1 (thiamine), B2 (riboflavin), B3 (niacin), B5 (pantothenic acid), B6 (pyridoxine), B7 (biotin), B9 (folic acid), B12 (cobalamin), C (ascorbic acid))
- minerals
	- components of tissues including bones and life supporting systems
	- major minerals needed in large amounts: calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, sodium, potassium, sulfur, and chloride
	- trace minerals, needed in small amounts: iron, zinc, iodine, copper, and manganese
	
As a general guide: **5% DV or less of sodium per serving is considered low, and 20% DV or more of sodium per serving is considered high.**

Fibers - complex non digestible carbs
- two types of fiber: soluble and insoluble
	Insoluble: speeds digestive system
	Soluble: absorbs cholesterol, **lowering cholesterol**

*if you take anything away from this class, eat more soluble fibers (avocados, beans, bananas, strawberries) to lower cholesterol*

Digestion: converts chemics in food into absorbable molecules
Absorption: transports digested molecules from GI into interior of body
### Gastrointestinal tract
mouth:
pharynx:
esophabus: moves food to stomach
stomach: secretes acid and protein-digesting enzyme
small intestine: absorbs nutrients into blood or lymph
large intestine: absorbs water and some vitamins and minerals

Salivary glands: produces saliva, a starch-digesting enzyme
liver: makes bile, aiding in digestion and absorption of fat
pancreas: produces enzymes to digest carbs, protein, and, fat
gall bladder: stores bile and releases into small intestine

## Metabolism
A series of reactions that transform chemicals in food into metabolites used for energy 

Catabolism: breaks down food chemicals to be absorbed
Anabolism: generates complex structures from simple molecules
Amino Acids -> protein, fatty acids -> triglyceride

*Body proteins cannot be synthesized unless diet contains all the essential amino acids*
